Cursor helps programmers turn ideas into software with AI. I understand "Cursor for everyone" as giving similar help to ordinary users. They can use everyday language to get things done.

I learned a lot there. I was the only Android engineer. We launched the first version in four weeks. I also worked on the RAG system. I found that I really enjoy turning new technology into a working product in a startup.

## 12C. Tell me about a project that did not meet your expectations.

I'd talk about the AI homework-grading product at Kezhi. I was responsible for Android and the multi-subject RAG work.

The first version was live. It could grade an assignment in a few seconds. But it did not yet give teachers enough help with targeted teaching.

It handled one assignment at a time. It did not group and study each student's past mistakes. Teachers could see today's wrong answers. But they could not easily see repeated mistakes or decide what to teach next.

I hadn't thought enough about how teachers would use the results after grading. Fast grading was useful. But teachers also needed a clear picture across several assignments.

If I planned it again, I'd include past mistakes and group them by knowledge point from the start. Then I'd add summaries of weak areas. I'd check the quality of the analysis, whether teachers used it and whether students made fewer similar mistakes.

The lesson was to think through the full workflow. After AI gives a result, how does it help the user take the next step?
